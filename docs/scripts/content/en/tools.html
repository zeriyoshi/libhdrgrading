The tools are small programs over the library's public API. hdrg-cli, hdrg-video, hdrg-ffmpeg and hdrg-inspect in {{src:tools}} are installed; hdrg-gui ({{src:tools/gui}}) is built on request and not installed. Two more for developers live in {{src:tests/tools}}: they read internal headers and are not installed.

{{fig:tools}}

hdrg-cli: still images

{{src:tools/hdrg-cli.c}} runs {{api:hdrg_convert}} with every setting as an option (and --set name=value by the library's names; the three CLIs parse --set and the enumerated options alike, and hdrg-cli and hdrg-video the filters, --filter and --filter-opt: {{src:tools/common.c}}). The writer is --format's (an id such as hdrg.jxl, or its name or extension; --jpeg, --jxl, --avif, --heif the same), else the output's extension's (.jxl, .avif, .heic), else JPEG; --format-opt key=value gives it an option and --list-formats lists the codecs and their options. The AVIF and HEIF modules are loaded from beside the program.

hdrg-cli --stats in.jpg out.jpg                 # gain-map JPEG (the default)
hdrg-cli --hybrid in.jpg out.jpg                # PQ base + SDR-recovering gain map
hdrg-cli --pq in.jpg out.jpg                    # single-layer PQ
hdrg-cli in.jpg out.jxl                         # JPEG XL: PQ base + SDR-recovering gain map
hdrg-cli --format-opt quality=95 in.jpg out.jxl # an option of the writer
hdrg-cli --preset anime in.png out.avif         # 10-bit AVIF (the module), the ANIME starting point
hdrg-cli --output-size 3840x0 in.jpg out.jpg    # the SCALE stage
hdrg-cli --plugin ./my.so --filter scale=org.example.scaler --filter-opt taps=4 in.jpg out.jpg

The options are the settings' names (--diffuse-max, --apl-peak-fraction, --peak-nits, --effort, --deband, …), plus --gpu auto|filters, --threads, --list-filters and --stats.

hdrg-video: frames through pipes

{{src:tools/hdrg-video.c}} runs a session over a y4m stream (or raw RGB with --size) and writes PQ (raw RGB, or --y4m for 4:2:0 10-bit BT.2020). It exposes the session's options: --temporal (two-pass reads the input twice), --latency, --past / --future, --proxy, --out-rate, --output-size, --filter, --gpu, --shed, --set name=value. It prints the latency report and MaxCLL / MaxFALL in x265's form, and can write HDR10+ JSON for x265 (--hdr10plus-json), ST 2094-50 JSON and a .cube LUT of the STATIC mapping (--export-lut).

ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -f yuv4mpegpipe -strict -1 - | hdrg-video --y4m - - |
  ffmpeg -i - -color_primaries bt2020 -color_trc smpte2084 -colorspace bt2020nc -c:v libx265 out.mp4

hdrg-ffmpeg: files

{{src:tools/hdrg-ffmpeg.c}} demuxes, decodes, grades and encodes a file with FFmpeg's libraries (dynamically linked) and {{src:tools/ffmpeg}}: 4:2:0 10-bit PQ / BT.2020 (libsvtav1 by default, else libx265), the mastering display on the stream, HDR10+ and ST 2094-50 on the packets, audio copied. AV1 film grain is taken from the decoder as parameters and put back into the PQ output by the session; --hwaccel videotoolbox with a *_videotoolbox encoder passes pixel buffers on the GPU when the session keeps its frames there (--latency playback or low), and under the default --latency offline maps them for the CPU in place, without a copy.

hdrg-gui

{{src:tools/gui}} (CMake HDRG_BUILD_GUI=ON): SDL3 and Dear ImGui. It opens stills and videos with the OS's decoders (AVFoundation / ImageIO, Media Foundation / WIC, GStreamer, or on Linux FFmpeg with HDRG_GUI_MEDIA=ffmpeg), plays them in an HDR window (extended-linear swapchain; the window's headroom becomes the display caps) as the graded HDR, the SDR original or both side by side (the SDR left, the HDR right), edits every setting (the fields are made from the settings table) and exports stills (by a format of its settings or the file's extension: JPEG, JPEG XL, and AVIF / HEIF with their modules beside it) and videos: HEVC 10-bit PQ without audio through VideoToolbox (macOS) or Media Foundation (Windows); on Linux, the FFmpeg build runs hdrg-ffmpeg (libsvtav1, else libx265, the audio copied) and the GStreamer build exports stills only.
